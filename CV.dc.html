Netflix Customer Service ObservabilityNetflix
Case Study →
Project summary
Users of a Netflix streaming observability tool were reporting difficultly in navigation and fragmentation when running investigations. The team supporting the system needed to prepare for both platform scale and the addition of AI for productivity enhancements.
Decisions & trade-offs
  • Find the Right Balance. We preserved the most frequently used navigation items in the their existing locations and avoided disorienting experienced users, while reorganizing lower visibility logs and utilities into more logical groupings.
  • Approach to AI. Assistant features were designed progressively as Engineers and Data Scientists had more tested functionality to offer: (1) RAG-style help (2) context-aware searches (3) automated investigations.
Outcomes
The overall outcome was a cleaner, more understandable experience with reduced cognitive load that still supported advanced workflows across multiple teams.
6sense Marketing Automation6sense
Case Study →
Project summary
The existing product provided simple wizards to allow any platform user the ability to automate marketing-related tasks on a daily or weekly cadence but the product’s adoption and engagement was much lower than expected.
Decisions & trade-offs
  • The decision tree canvas format would provide many benefits over other formats (users can start simple, scale as needed, and can branch logic in multiple ways).
  • The model removes barriers toward adoption and allows customers to start small and scale as they become more comfortable with the app.
  • I started minimalist with visual design until we had customer feedback telling us otherwise.
Outcomes
It provided a path toward better adoption, increased revenue, allowed users to start small and scale as needed, and came with the added benefit of simplifying the overall platform experience .
Oracle New Platform for Ops & CSMsOracle
Case Study →
Project summary
The primary focus of CSMs was related to monitoring customer usage and helping them throughout the contract period. However, their primary tools were spreadsheets and half a dozen other internal tools. They needed one system to bring it all together, prevent churn, and help Oracle compete against Amazon, Microsoft, and Google.
Decisions & trade-offs
  • Leverage existing tools and patterns where possible, before creating your own (Oracle JET javascript framework, which included responsive components, and ALTA UI visual libraries).
  • Focus on simple default views that can be accessed with very few clicks.
  • Moved to a horizontal nav to gain width for tablets (landscape orientation)
Outcomes
  • Adoption of 4000 users in one year
  • One source of truth for all data (no more spreadsheets)
  • Reduced time to onboard
Netflix Developer PortalNetflix
Case Study →
Project summary
Netflix engineers supporting common customer flows (sign up, account management, payment flow, etc.) found it difficult to find simple answers to questions related to the most recent version of APIs and docs related to the product context they were currently working in.
Decisions & trade-offs
We established an information architecture to match groups initially, which fit the current siloed ownership. Once most groups had begun to adopt and engage with the portal, we could plan an IA shift toward organizing the navigation around product context (more scalable for the future - and “reorg proof”).
Outcomes
The team created a brand new AI-driven developer API and doc portal to act as a front door to all other resources. This would provide curated APIs and content and reclaim countless hours of lost productivity time.

Mentoring

I’ve been lucky enough to have worked with a number of talented designers over the years, seeing them grow from junior or senior level to lead or director levels.

I always attempt to lead with the following considerations in mind:

  • Provide clarity in business needs, goals, and prioritization.
  • Create an ‘open door’ policy that provides a safe place to question anything at anytime.
  • Make sure designers have the information and resources they need to do their job effectively.
  • Help them to home in on the real problems we’re solving and not get lost in “shiny new toys” as we attempt to innovate.
