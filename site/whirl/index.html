01 · BASIC PROCESS

Seven steps from problem to measured impact, then back around

Steps 1-2 build understanding. Step 3 is design. Steps 4-5 are development and final review. Steps 6-7 close the loop with data and feedback.

01 · Understand problem and impact

Problem framing and user research

  • Gather early context and list the questions that need answers.
  • Group research into themes and apply 5W1H: who, what, when, where, why, how.
  • Prioritize problems by impact and give the team a grounded reference.
Output: a prioritized problem statement the team agrees on.
02 · Understand problem and impact

Context and preliminary work

  • Clarify what is needed before drawing screens.
  • Map user flows, requirements, constraints and dependencies with product and engineering.
  • Agree on what success looks like.
Output: scope, flows and open questions resolved.
03 · Design

Explore options and narrow to one approach

  • Start from existing patterns and explore a couple of options.
  • Select one approach and state its benefits and trade-offs as you go.
  • Keep the options few so decisions stay fast.
Output: one approach with its rationale written down.
04 · Develop and final review

Build and validate

  • Build a working version early and test it, preferably with customers.
  • Work with engineering during the build to resolve gaps as they appear.
  • Revise the approach when validation says so.
Output: a validated build and the changes it requires.
05 · Develop and final review

Polish and ship

  • Refine details: spacing, states, copy and accessibility.
  • Run a final review against the agreed approach.
  • Release.
Output: a shipped release.
06 · Measure

Measure

  • Adoption: are people using it?
  • Engagement: how often and how deeply?
  • User feedback: what do telemetry and further research say?
Output: evidence compared against the success criteria from step 2.
07 · Repeat

Continue to optimize with user feedback

Findings from measurement become the inputs to the next round of problem framing.
THE PROCESS CAN BE COMPRESSED
When context is known and relevant patterns are available, the steps shorten.
Context is known: steps 1-2 shrink to confirming what is already understood.
Patterns exist: step 3 starts from them, with fewer options to weigh.

02 · BENEFITS OF THE DESIGN-TO-CODE PIPELINE

Review on a real, production-ready page

Handoffs, design system updates and guideline writing have always taken repeated reviews. One simple format can improve several of these at once.

PART 1
The design
A Figma file, a screenshot, or a prompt in Claude Design.
PART 2
The MCP server
Maintains the design system and applies the correct tokens, components, layouts, behaviors and accessibility to any design sent to it.
PART 3
The staging server
The endpoint for designers. With an engineer's help, they go from idea to production-ready React pages quickly.
A single source of truth
Designers and developers work from the same rules. The MCP server moves AI from fully probabilistic output toward deterministic, grounded output.
Fewer review loops
The team reviews a real page instead of a Figma simulation. The design system is managed on the MCP server instead of through Figma, Storybook and production.
Time for higher-level patterns
Saved time goes to reusable patterns for flows and pages, which match how product, engineers and users think.

03 · GROUNDING THE TEAM

How we can help the team make better decisions

Design critique tends to fail without a frame of reference. A shared set of questions and reference artifacts gives the team one.

GROUNDING

Questions we should always be able to answer.

Not an audit — a shared checklist. Where an answer is thin, that's simply the next thing to explore.

5W1H
WHO
Which personas actually use the product every day?
WHAT
What business outcomes are we driving?
What's the competitive differentiator?
What cross-product opportunities exist?
WHEN
What are the roadmap highlights for the next 6 months?
WHERE
What are the constraints (permissions, state, failure modes, etc.)?
Are we designing the platform for scale?
WHY
Why are we building this (value ad, pain points addressed, etc)?
Why are deals won or lost — and what causes churn?
Are decisions grounded in data and made from a customer-first perspective?
Are we mapping design decisions back to revenue?
HOW
How does the company actually make money with this?
Are we reducing cognitive load?
What are the trade-offs (i.e.. what are we optimizing for)?
Are designs reducing support tickets?
USABILITY
PERCEIVE
Is there a clear entry point?
Does spacing group what belongs together?
Is every element earning its place?
THINK
Are the choices few and ranked?
Is information chunked, not dumped?
Does it match patterns users know?
ACT
Are key targets close and sized appropriately?
Does every action give feedback?
Are mistakes prevented and reversible?
